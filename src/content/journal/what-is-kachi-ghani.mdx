"Kachi ghani" literally means a raw press. A wooden pestle (the kolhu) turns slowly inside a wooden mortar and crushes the seeds. Because it turns slowly, the oil stays cool. Nothing is cooked out of it.

## Wood-pressed vs cold-pressed

Both words try to say the same thing: the oil was pressed without external heat. "Cold-pressed" is often used for steel expellers that run cooler than industrial ones. "Wood-pressed" means the traditional wooden ghani. We call our oils wood-pressed because that is what we use.

## What about refined oil?

Refined oil is usually extracted at high temperature, often with chemical solvents, and then bleached and deodorised so that it looks clear and smells of nothing. That process also removes much of what made the seed worth eating.

## How to tell the difference at home

- Kachi ghani oil smells and tastes strongly of its seed.
- It may carry fine sediment at the bottom of the bottle.
- Some oils (coconut, groundnut) thicken or turn cloudy in winter.

None of these are defects. They are signs the oil has been left alone.
